# Current-AI Patterns (Dated, Replace Wholesale)

This is the dated layer of the skill. Every pattern here is tied to a specific model version (GPT-5.x, Claude 4.5-5). These reflexes move every model cycle; the skill itself documents how they shifted between 2024 and 2026. Treat this file as replaceable wholesale: when the models change, rewrite it. The evergreen guidance lives in the other references; this part has a shelf life of months.

## What Changed by Mid-2026

The surface patterns moved. OpenAI suppressed em dashes in GPT-5.1, and signature words ("delve," "tapestry," "vibrant," "myriad") have been trained out of the newest Claude models. A clean vocabulary no longer makes writing read as human; the structural patterns are what give it away (see `references/structural-patterns.md`).

Punctuation shifted toward Claude. Claude leaned hard on the em dash (its worst tell) and overused colons and semicolons too, while GPT-5.1+ output ran nearly dash-free. This is why an em dash now reads as an AI tell to attentive readers even though it is fine writing in the abstract. Keep the zero-dash rule and watch colon density.

## Current Aidiolects (updated mid-2026)

Different LLMs have different "aidiolects." The patterns below reflect GPT-5.x and Claude 4.5-5 era output. The words each model overuses are also in `references/vocabulary-banlist.md`; here they characterize the dialect.

### ChatGPT (GPT-5 / 5.1 / 5.2), the "Motivator" dialect

- Em dashes suppressed since 5.1, so a dash-free draft can still read as pure GPT. What remains:
- The negated contrast, roughly one per paragraph: "It's not just X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X. It's about Y." Still the single most GPT-characteristic sentence shape.
- Symmetric two-clause hooks opening posts: "Most people think X. The reality is Y." / "Forget X. Focus on Y." Fine once; a fingerprint when it opens four out of five pieces.
- Rigid "Firstly / Secondly / Finally" scaffolding and intro-triplet-recap arcs.
- Hedging verbs used as padding: "ensuring" (the strongest single padding word in current AI), "ensures," "highlights," "supports," "reflects." A human says what the thing does.
- The top AI trigram: "plays a [crucial/critical/important] role in shaping." Delete on sight.
- Intensifier adverbs without evidence: "significantly," "effectively," "directly," "increasingly." If no number backs it, cut it.
- "rather than" overuse (one of the highest-leverage multi-word edits); "capable of X" instead of "able to X"; "conversely" where "but" would do.
- Assertive overconfident declaratives: "Here's the truth about Z." "The best founders know Y."
- A "sanitized" texture: GPT-5's self-correction pass scrubs obvious AI-isms but leaves prose that feels cleaned, with no awkward transitions at all. Perfectly smooth = suspicious.
- Boilerplate closers: "As X continues to evolve, one thing is clear...," "the key takeaway is..." Any ending built on "one thing is clear" or "the key takeaway" is a model ending.

### Claude (Sonnet 4.5/4.6, Opus 4.5, Claude 5), the "Philosopher" dialect

- Worst punctuation offender of 2026: the em dash (mid-sentence, additive), with colons and semicolons close behind.
- Hedge-and-reassure stacking, sometimes three hedges before saying anything: "While this may vary, generally speaking, in most cases, it's worth noting that..."
- Signature vocabulary: "worth noting," "nuanced," "comprehensive," "fundamentally," "paradigm," "in essence," "essentially," "inherent tensions," "thoughtful approach," "this raises important questions about."
- Hedging adverbs, fine alone but a tell when clustered: "typically," "often," "sometimes," "potentially," "usually."
- Empathetic framing on autopilot: "understandably, many people feel...," "this can be frustrating for..."
- Essayistic arc regardless of format: contextualize the question, explore multiple perspectives, add a qualification, close by observing what the analysis "raises" rather than concluding what it means. LinkedIn post or pricing memo, same Hegelian dialectic.
- Abstract vocabulary inflation: "founders with strong metacognitive awareness often find that" where a human writes "the best founders know."
- Starting sentences with "And" / "But" as a flow crutch every other paragraph. Occasionally human; as a system, a tell.
- Bimodal sentence rhythm and paragraph over-fragmentation (see `references/structural-patterns.md`).
- Note: "delve," "tapestry," "vibrant," "myriad" are largely absent from newest Claude output. A draft can be free of them and still read as Claude; check the structural patterns instead.

### Gemini

Purple prose, excessive adjectives, moralizing, explicit theme statements, textbook "Educator" tone.
